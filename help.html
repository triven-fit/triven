Help guide

How to get the most out of Triven: a free training log for swimming, cycling, running and multisport.

Getting started

  1. Create an account on the sign-in page and confirm your email address.
  2. Set up your profile. The first time you sign in, Settings opens. Choose the name other athletes see and your username (your profile's address, triven.fit/username), add a photo and pick your privacy (see Privacy and sharing). Choose metric or imperial units, and enter your date of birth, weight and threshold heart rates if you know them; your zones and training load depend on them. Triathletes: add your swim threshold (CSS) too.
  3. Bring in your history. Upload your Garmin export or individual files (see Getting activities in).
  4. Connect your watch so new activities arrive by themselves.
  5. Find your people. Follow team mates and join your club on the People tab.

Install Triven on your phone or computer

There's no app store download: Triven installs straight from your browser.

  • iPhone or iPad: open triven.fit in Chrome or Safari, tap the Share button (in Chrome it's in the address bar; in Safari, in the toolbar), then Add to Home Screen and Add.
  • Android: open triven.fit in Chrome and tap Install app when it pops up, or the menu (⋮) then Add to home screen and Install.
  • Mac or Windows with Chrome or Edge: open triven.fit, click Install app at the top of the page (next to your name), then Install. If you don't see the button, use the install icon at the right of the address bar, or in Chrome the menu (⋮) → Cast, save and share → Install page as app.
  • Mac with Safari (macOS Sonoma or later): File → Add to Dock, or the Share button → Add to Dock.

It then opens from its own icon, in its own window or full screen, and always runs the latest version. On a phone, Help and Sign out are at the top of Settings.

Getting activities in

Upload files

On the Upload tab, drop in .fit files or a .zip of them. Triven skips anything it already has, so it's safe to upload the same files twice.

Your whole Garmin history

  1. Sign in at Garmin Connect on the web, open your account's Data Management section and choose Export your data.
  2. Garmin emails you a download link, usually within a few days.
  3. Drop the zip on the Upload tab and leave the tab open until it says Done.
A Garmin export holds thousands of files that aren't workouts: daily steps, sleep, heart-rate monitoring and device settings. Triven lists these as Not an activity (skipped). That's expected; the summary at the top counts how many activities were added.

Automatic sync

Triven collects new activities through intervals.icu, a free service that connects to Garmin, COROS, Suunto, Wahoo and others.

  1. Create a free intervals.icu account and connect your watch there.
  2. In intervals.icu settings, copy your athlete ID and create an API key.
  3. In Triven, open Settings → Connected services and paste both in.

New activities are fetched when you open Triven, and otherwise within about half an hour (less often if you haven't opened the app for a few weeks). Sync now in Settings fetches them straight away. Apple Watch users can send workouts to intervals.icu with the HealthFit app.

Without a file

Log without a file on the Upload tab records a session by hand: sport, time and distance, with legs for multisport.

Multisport

A triathlon, duathlon, aquathlon, aquabike, brick or swimrun recorded in multisport mode on your watch comes in as one activity, with each leg and the transitions in between. Triven names the race type and distance class, for example "Sprint triathlon".

Joining separate files into one race

If you stopped and started the watch between legs, you have separate files. Triven offers to combine files that follow on within 10 minutes of each other when you upload them. You can also do it any time: open one of the activities and click Link activities, then pick the others (up to five, within 12 hours). The gaps between them become transitions. Unlink splits them again.

Leg types

In Edit you can set each leg's type: treadmill, track, trail or road runs; indoor, virtual, gravel, mountain-bike or road rides; pool or open-water swims. Treadmill legs can have their distance corrected, and track runs are snapped to a 400 m track.

Swimming

Pool swims get their own card on the activity page, built from every length your watch recorded:

  • Pace on swimming time. Rests between sets never slow your pace, even if your watch lumped several lengths into one lap. The page shows swim time and rest separately.
  • Sets and lengths. Each set between rests, with its stroke, time, pace per 100 m, SWOLF and strokes. Click a set to see each length.
  • SWOLF is a length's time in seconds plus its stroke count. Lower is more efficient. Compare it only within the same pool length.
  • Stroke rate is counted the way your watch counts it: one stroke each time the arm wearing it completes a cycle. On freestyle and backstroke that's half your total arm pulls, so double it to compare with counters that count both arms.
  • Swim zones: time in five zones around your swim threshold (CSS), for pool swims (length by length) and open water. Set your CSS in Settings → Swimming.
  • Swim bests at 100, 200, 400, 750, 800 and 1500 m, 1.9 km and 3.8 km. In the pool they count only continuous swimming, so a 400 m best is a real 400 m, not four 100s with breaks. Open-water swims use GPS distance. Drill lengths don't count.

Swim threshold (CSS)

CSS, critical swim speed, is the pace per 100 m you could hold for about 30 minutes: your swimming threshold. To find it, swim a hard 400 m, rest, then a hard 200 m. In Settings → Swimming, enter both times and Triven works it out: CSS = (400 m time − 200 m time) ÷ 2. CSS sets your swim zones and your swim training load, which comes from your pace against CSS (heart rate in the water is unreliable). Like other thresholds, it's kept by date.

Activity pages

  • Sections bar: click a leg, lap or flag to zoom the charts and map to it.
  • Charts: elevation, pace or speed, heart rate, power, cadence and more. Runs also get grade-adjusted pace (the pace on the flat that would cost the same effort, worked out from the gradient) and stride length (from your watch's running dynamics, or estimated from speed and cadence).
  • Weather: outdoor activities show the temperature, feels-like temperature, humidity and wind at the time. Runs add a heat cost: how much the heat and humidity slow you down, and your heat-adjusted pace.
  • Race history: on a race, your other races at the same event (same start area and distance) side by side, with every leg and transition.
  • Training focus: time in each heart-rate zone, and what the session mostly trained.
  • FTP and power: best power for each duration, from a power meter or estimated from speed and gradient. The FTP used is the one in your Settings for that day, or the ride's own estimate; the FTP stored on your watch isn't used, because it's often a default nobody set.
  • Running balance: left/right ground contact time is on its own chart, with your balanced range shaded and stretches outside it flagged on the chart and map.
  • Edit menu: Edit details (name, description, private notes, leg types, gear, photos and videos, and what you share), Mark as race, Trim (cut off the start or end, for example when you forgot to stop the watch and drove home; Triven warns you when an activity looks as if it includes driving), Link activities and Make a relay.
  • Share: copies a short link to the activity, like triven.fit/carlg/412, or opens your phone's share sheet. It opens for Triven members who are allowed to see the activity.
  • Instagram post: makes a picture of the activity to share.
  • Recording device: the watch or bike computer that recorded the activity is shown under its name, on the feed, on the activity page, on shared pictures and in your data export. Garmin devices appear as "Garmin" plus the model, as Garmin asks of apps that show its data.

People and following

On the People tab, search for athletes by name and follow them. If someone approves followers, your request waits until they accept. If you approve followers, people asking to follow you appear on the same tab. The feed has four views:

  • Me: your activities.
  • Following: people you follow.
  • Club: you and your club mates.
  • Everyone: public activities from all Triven athletes.

Give kudos and comment on anything you can see.

Notifications

The bell at the top shows what's new: kudos and comments on your activities, replies after you've commented on someone else's, new followers and follow requests, relay invitations, join requests for clubs you run, and posts in your clubs. The number is how many arrived since you last opened it; opening the list marks them read on all your devices. Click one to go straight there. The list covers the last 60 days.

Profiles and links

Everyone has a profile at triven.fit/username: name, clubs, this year's totals, best efforts and activities, showing only what you're allowed to see. Click a name in the feed to open it, or your own name at the top. Change your username in Settings; old links keep working. Links to profiles and activities open only for signed-in Triven members.

Clubs

Joining

Search for a club on the People tab, or open an invite link from a member. Some clubs let you straight in; others send your request to the club's admins.

Starting a club

Click Start a club on the People tab. You choose:

  • Listed (anyone can find it in search) or invite only (only people with the link).
  • Free to join or admin approval.

Either way, the club has an invite link: Copy invite link on the club page. You can change these settings later.

Running a club

  • Club news: admins post messages that every member sees on the club page.
  • Photo and description: click Add photo under the club badge; Settings changes the rest.
  • Requests: approve or decline them in the panel beside the news.
  • Members: the owner can make members admins, hand the club over, or remove people. Admins can remove members.
  • New invite link: replaces the link, so the old one stops working.

If the owner leaves, the longest-serving admin takes over. A club with no members left is deleted.

Leaderboard

Each club page ranks its members for this week, month or year, by distance, time or elevation, for all sports or one. Multisport races count each leg towards its sport. Only activities shared with the club count: private ones never do.

Relays

In a relay, each person does one leg: one swims, another bikes, another runs. Triven joins your activities into one relay that appears on every member's feed, while each activity stays in its owner's own log for totals, bests and training load.

  1. Open your leg's activity and click Make a relay.
  2. Pick a team mate's activity from within 12 hours of yours, or let them choose it. Relays are between people who follow each other.
  3. They see an invitation at the top of their feed and accept it with their activity, or decline.

The relay page shows each leg, the hand-overs between them and the total time from the first start to the last finish. Any member can add another leg (up to six), rename the relay or leave it. Each leg's sport, time and distance are shown to anyone who can see the relay; everything else follows the owner's own sharing settings.

Bests and trophies

Triven finds your best efforts inside every activity:

  • Running: 400 m up to the marathon, then 50 km, 50 miles, 100 km and 100 miles.
  • Cycling: 10, 25 and 50-mile time trials, 90 km (70.3 bike), 100 km, 100 miles and 180 km (Ironman bike), longest ride, most elevation, and best power from 1 second to 1 hour.
  • Swimming: 100, 200, 400, 750, 800 and 1500 m, 1.9 km and 3.8 km (pool bests count continuous swimming only; see Swimming).

Training log → Best efforts lists every category in distance or duration order, for all time or one year. Click one to see your top 10, each from a different activity. Open an activity from the list and a link at the top takes you back to it.

Spurious numbers (a power spike, a GPS jump): click Ignore next to the effort. It then stops counting everywhere: bests, trophies, your profile and FTP suggestions. The activity itself isn't changed, and Restore brings the effort back.

An activity page shows a gold, silver or bronze trophy when an effort was your best, second-best or third-best ever at that distance or power duration, as things stood on that day. A trophy stays on the activity even after you beat it. Trophies are also given per year: a cup marked '25 means 1st, 2nd or 3rd of 2025. Treadmill and indoor legs don't count for distance bests.

Training log

  • Totals: distance, time and elevation by sport for a week, month, year or dates you choose, with a chart grouped to suit the period (daily, weekly, monthly or yearly). Walks, hikes, gym sessions and anything that isn't a swim, ride or run count under Other.
  • Calendar: every day's activities with their training load.
  • Fitness and form: load is scored on the familiar TSS scale. Fitness is your 42-day average load, fatigue your 7-day average, and form is fitness minus fatigue (negative when you're carrying fatigue, positive when fresh).
  • Training status: productive, maintaining, recovery, peaking, overreaching or detraining, from how your load is changing. It needs about six weeks of activities with heart rate.
  • Thresholds: suggestions for threshold heart rate, threshold pace and FTP from your hardest recent efforts. One click applies a suggestion from the day of the activity that showed it; earlier activities keep their old values.
  • Race predictor: 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon times from your best running efforts of the last 12 weeks.
  • Activities: search everything by name, notes, gear, type ("treadmill", "race", "sprint triathlon") or month, filter by sport, date and distance, sort, and see totals for what matches. Press / from anywhere, or the magnifier at the top.

Races

Training log → Races holds your season. Add each race with its date, type, distance, a goal time and a priority: A (the one that matters), B (important, short taper) or C (a training race). For each one you get:

  • A countdown, also shown for your next race on the feed sidebar.
  • Your race-day form if you keep training as you are.
  • For A and B races, how much to cut training in the final days (10 for A, 5 for B) to arrive fresh, with form at +5 or more.

After the race, link the activity as your result to see it against your goal.

Goals

Set weekly, monthly and annual goals for each sport or all sports together, by distance, time or elevation, from Set goals on the feed sidebar. Monthly and annual goals show a marker for where you'd be by today at an even rate, and how far ahead or behind you are.

Triven doesn't estimate VO₂ max: your watch already gives you one, and a second figure would only disagree with it.

Gear

Add your shoes and bikes on the Gear tab to track their distance, with a replacement reminder per item. Set defaults by role (everyday, trail, track, treadmill or races for shoes; everyday, indoor, gravel, mountain bike or races for bikes) and new activities pick the right one. Marking an activity as a race switches it to your race gear. In multisport activities, each leg has its own gear.

Each item has an In use from date. Default gear only goes on activities from that date, so importing years of history doesn't put today's shoes on old runs. When you set or change the date, Triven offers to remove the item from activities before it.

Settings

  • Profile and privacy: name, username, photo, date of birth (only you see it), weight (typed in, no smart scale needed), units (metric or imperial), default visibility for new uploads, search, follower approval and who can see your activities.
  • Connected services: intervals.icu for automatic sync.
  • Heart rate: run and bike threshold heart rate (enter lab or field-test results here), max and resting heart rate, and zone boundaries.
  • Running form: the left/right balance range that gets flagged.
  • Cycling: FTP, bike weight, riding position and tyres, used to estimate power on rides without a power meter.
  • Swimming: your swim threshold (CSS), or a 400 m and 200 m time trial to work it out.

Thresholds over time

Triven keeps a dated history of your threshold heart rates, max and resting heart rate, FTP, swim threshold and weight. Every activity is worked out with the values in effect on its day: if your FTP was 240 a year ago and is 270 now, last year's rides still use 240. When you change a value, choose From a date (today, or the day of a lab test) or For past activities too. For days before your first change, Triven uses the heart rate thresholds and weight your watch recorded in that activity's file, if any (never the watch's FTP). The activity page says which values it used and where they came from.

Suggest a feature or report a problem

Use Feedback at the top of the app, or on a phone, the links at the top of Settings: Suggest a feature or Report a problem. It goes straight to the person who builds Triven.

  • Problem reports include the page you were on, the app version, your browser and screen size, so the problem can be found. Describe what happened and what you expected; steps to make it happen again help most.
  • You'll get a notification when your feedback's status changes (seen, planned, done), and any reply shows under What you've sent in the feedback form.

Questions

An activity is missing.

If you use automatic sync, click Sync now in Settings and check the activity reached intervals.icu. Activities that reached intervals.icu from Strava aren't imported, under Strava's terms; upload the original file instead.

My zones or load look wrong.

Check your threshold and max heart rates in Settings. The activity page's training-focus note shows which values it used for that day and where they came from (your setting or your watch). To correct a past period, add a change From the right date.

How do I delete my account?

Settings → Your data → Delete my account. Type delete, press the button, then confirm the final warning, and everything goes straight away: activities, original files, photos, gear, comments, kudos and follows. Clubs you run pass to the next admin or member. It can't be undone, so use Download my data first if you want a copy.

Can I get a copy of my data?

Yes: Settings → Your data → Download my data gives you a zip with your profile and settings, every activity (as a spreadsheet and in full detail), gear, bests, comments, follows and clubs. Tick Include original activity files and photos to add your .fit files and pictures too; that takes longer with a big history.
